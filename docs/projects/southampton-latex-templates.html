Southampton LaTeX Templates

First published
24 July 2024
Last updated
3 October 2026
Repository
sotonlatex

A collection of University of Southampton LaTeX templates I put together in 2024: a PhD thesis template, a Beamer presentation template, a conference poster template and a short report template. Each one lives in its own folder and compiles on its own, and they all upload straight to Overleaf.

I wrote these to make my own life easier. The poster and Beamer templates share the same underlying style, with the same colours, fonts and Southampton branding, so I only had to keep one figures folder and could move content between a talk and a poster. The only real rework was resizing figures for the aspect ratio or the width of the slide or page.

The thesis template

The thesis template follows the standard Southampton thesis layout, with the university crest on the title page and the traditional front matter. On top of that it uses the glossaries package to build lists automatically: acronyms, non-dimensional numbers (Reynolds, Mach, Prandtl and so on) and mathematical symbols. Only entries you actually use end up in the final lists, so they stay in sync as chapters come and go.

The final version of the thesis template I actually built for a friend. They wanted the glossary and the list of symbols combined, so every symbol behaves like a normal glossary entry. It took me a couple of days to get right, but it's quite a nifty thing: you can hyperlink pretty much every symbol in the thesis, just like \gls does for a glossary term, so clicking a symbol in an equation takes you straight to its definition and units.

Using them

The easiest way is to upload the folder you need to Overleaf as a new project, which handles the glossary and bibliography passes for you. Locally they build with pdflatex; the Beamer template needs TeX Live 2023 or later. Everything is in the GitHub repository under the MIT licence (university logos and branding excluded), and feel free to get in touch if you'd like me to share them with you directly on Overleaf.
