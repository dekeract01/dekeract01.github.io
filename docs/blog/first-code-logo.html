Turtle Logo, first code

First published
2021
Last updated
14 October 2026

I actually wrote about this back in 2021, but since I'm moving everything over to this new site, I thought it was worth writing it up again. It's about the very first bit of coding I ever did. I remember doing this back in Year 4, in a computer class at school on Windows 98, and looking back, this is where it all started: the curiosity about how a few simple commands can make a computer do things.

So in this post, I'll go through what Logo actually is, show a few of the things I recreated from memory, and there's a little turtle further down that you can play with yourself, right here in your browser. No installing anything.

If you've never come across it, Logo is the one with the turtle. You've got a little turtle on the screen, you give it simple commands like move forward 100 steps or turn right 90 degrees, and it draws a line wherever it goes. That's pretty much it, but it's surprising how far you can get with just that.

Drawing a square, for example, is just this:

repeat 4 [forward 100 right 90]

And once you've got a square, you can spin it round to get something that looks far more impressive than it has any right to:

repeat 36 [repeat 4 [forward 100 right 90] right 10]

The best bit is that you can teach the turtle new words. Say you want a flower. First you teach it what a petal is, two curves that meet at the ends, then a flower is just twelve petals going round. Add a stem and a couple of leaves, and you've got this:

to petal
  repeat 2 [repeat 18 [forward 4 right 5] right 90]
end

to flower
  repeat 12 [petal right 30]
end

penup forward 40 pendown
setpencolor 10 setpensize 3
back 150
forward 50 petal left 90 petal right 90
forward 100
setpencolor "deeppink setpensize 2
flower

Once petal exists, you use it just like forward or right. That's the idea of breaking a big problem into small named pieces, which is basically what all programming is, and I was doing it back in Year 4 without realising.

Rather than just showing you, I've put a little turtle right here so you can have a go. Hit Run, change the numbers, see what happens. There are a few examples underneath too.

Try:

What can the turtle do?
  • forward 100, back 100 (or fd, bk)
  • right 90, left 90 (or rt, lt)
  • penup, pendown (or pu, pd) to move without drawing
  • repeat 4 [ ... ] to do something over and over
  • setpencolor "red or setpencolor 4, and setpensize 3
  • home, clearscreen (or cs), setxy 50 50, setheading 90
  • to square :size ... end to teach it a new word, then square 50
  • if :size > 100 [stop], make "n 10, random 360, repcount, print
  • Ctrl/Cmd + Enter runs your code

Honestly, I don't remember anything I actually made back then, so I recreated a few things as best I could, as a bit of a nostalgic trip. It was fun going back to it.

There's loads of turtle Logo stuff online if you fancy having a go yourself. jslogo runs Logo right in your browser, Turtle Academy has lessons and challenges, and FMSLogo is a free desktop version, very close to what we had at school. If you want the history, the Logo Foundation at MIT goes into where it all came from.

Looking back, I really do think starting with Logo is a great way to get into coding. You type a line, and straight away something happens on the screen. There's no setup, no compiling, nothing to get in the way. And that's what got me. I was genuinely amazed at what I could make with a few simple instructions, and that feeling has stuck with me ever since.
